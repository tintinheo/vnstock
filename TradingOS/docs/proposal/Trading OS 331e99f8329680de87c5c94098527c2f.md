## 🟢 MODULE 1: MARKET CYCLES (THE MACRO CONTEXT)

Before executing a trade, you must identify the current "System State."

### 1. The Wyckoff Market Cycle

The market moves through four distinct phases of institutional activity:

- **Accumulation:** Institutions "collect" shares at a base. Price moves sideways with low volume.
- **Markup:** The "breakout" phase. This is the highest ROI period where the trend is confirmed.
- **Distribution:** Institutions "unload" shares to late-comers. High volatility but no price progress.
- **Markdown:** The downtrend. System-wide liquidation. Stay in cash.

### 2. Elliott Wave Theory

Used to measure the "depth" of a trend:

- **Impulsive Waves (1, 3, 5):** The primary direction. **Wave 3** is typically the longest and strongest "surge."
- **Corrective Waves (2, 4):** Temporary pullbacks. **Wave 4** is often a complex sideways movement before the final Wave 5 thrust.

## 🔵 MODULE 2: BOTTOM IDENTIFICATION & ENTRY (THE "SPRING" & "GAP")

Finding the point of maximum exhaustion and institutional "footprints."

### 1. The Wyckoff Spring (The Shakeout)

A "trap" set to trigger stop-losses before a massive reversal.

- **Logic:** Price breaks a well-known Support level, triggers sell orders, then recovers instantly.
- **Validation:** The breakout below support must occur on **low volume** (Supply Exhaustion).

### 2. Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A price imbalance caused by an overwhelming surge of orders.

- **Definition:** A 3-candle sequence where the Wick of Candle 1 and Wick of Candle 3 do not overlap.
- **Strategy:** Treat the FVG as a "magnet." Wait for the price to retrace and **"fill"** at least 50% of the gap before entering.

### 3. Bullish Divergence

Identifying the "momentum-price" mismatch.

- **Condition:** Price creates a **Lower Low**, but the RSI (Relative Strength Index) creates a **Higher Low**.
- **Logic:** The "engine" is gaining strength even though the "vehicle" is still sliding.

---

## 🔴 MODULE 3: TOP IDENTIFICATION & EXIT (THE CLIMAX)

Knowing when the "CPU is overheating" and it’s time to exit.

### 1. Buying Climax

The final stage of a Markup.

- **Signs:** Vertical price movement, extreme "good news" in the media, and record-high volume with no further price appreciation.

### 2. Fibonacci Extensions

Mathematical targets for profit-taking.

- **Key Levels:**
    
    $$1.618$$
    
    and
    
    $$2.618$$
    
    .
    
- **Logic:** These levels represent the common expansion ratios where institutional algorithms are programmed to take profit.

### 3. Bearish Divergence

- **Condition:** Price creates a **Higher High**, but the RSI creates a **Lower High**.
- **Logic:** The trend is "gasping for air"—it's an unstable state ready for a crash.

## 🟡 MODULE 4: EXECUTION & QUANTITATIVE RISK

Applying logical constraints to the trade.

### 1. VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)

The "Fair Price" anchor for the day.

$$\text{VWAP} = \frac{\sum (\text{Price} \times \text{Volume})}{\sum \text{Volume}}$$

- **Entry Rule:** Only buy if
    
    $$\text{Price} > \text{VWAP}$$
    
    .
    
- **Exit Rule:** If
    
    $$\text{Price}$$
    
    crosses below
    
    $$\text{VWAP}$$
    
    on high volume, the "bullish bias" is broken.
    

### 2. Risk/Reward (R/R) Ratio

The CEO's decision-making threshold. Never enter a trade where the risk is greater than half the reward.

$$\text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Target Price} - \text{Entry Price}}{\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop-Loss}} \ge 2.0$$

import numpy as np

def analyze_market_signals(df):
    """
    df must contain: ['Open', 'High', 'Low', 'Close', 'Volume']
    """
    # 1. Calculate Intraday VWAP
    df['VWAP'] = (df['Close'] * df['Volume']).cumsum() / df['Volume'].cumsum()

    # 2. Identify Fair Value Gaps (FVG)
    # Bullish FVG: Low of candle[i+1] > High of candle[i-1]
    df['Bullish_FVG'] = np.where((df['Low'].shift(-1) > df['High'].shift(1)), 
                                 (df['Low'].shift(-1) - df['High'].shift(1)), 0)
    
    # Bearish FVG: High of candle[i+1] < Low of candle[i-1]
    df['Bearish_FVG'] = np.where((df['High'].shift(-1) < df['Low'].shift(1)), 
                                  (df['Low'].shift(1) - df['High'].shift(-1)), 0)

    # 3. RSI Calculation for Divergence
    window = 14
    delta = df['Close'].diff()
    gain = (delta.where(delta > 0, 0)).rolling(window=window).mean()
    loss = (-delta.where(delta < 0, 0)).rolling(window=window).mean()
    rs = gain / loss
    df['RSI'] = 100 - (100 / (1 + rs))

    return df

### Laws of Supply-Demand and the Three Wyckoff Laws

The core of Wyckoff analysis rests on three fundamental laws that can be quantified in the algorithm:

1. **The Law of Supply and Demand:** When demand is greater than supply, prices rise; when supply is greater than demand, prices fall. The "Trading OS" algorithm will scan order book data from the SSI API to identify these imbalances in real-time.
2. **The Law of Cause and Effect:** The length of an accumulation phase (Cause) determines the magnitude of the subsequent uptrend (Effect). We use Point-and-Figure charts or cumulative volume analysis to forecast price targets.
3. **The Law of Effort vs. Result:** Divergence between trading volume (Effort) and price movement (Result) often signals a trend reversal. For instance, if volume spikes but price fails to rise correspondingly, it is a sign of distribution.

**Các công thức Toán học (Formulas for AI Agent Coding):**

1. **Trend Filter (Bộ lọc Xu hướng):**
    
    $Trend = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } SMA(Close, 50) > SMA(Close, 200) \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
    
2. **Momentum Pullback (Nhịp kéo ngược):**
    
    Sử dụng Chỉ số Sức mạnh Tương đối (RSI).
    
    $RSI = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + RS} \quad \text{với} \quad RS = \frac{EMA(Gain, 14)}{EMA(Loss, 14)}$
    
    *Điều kiện mua:* $RSI(14)$ cắt lên từ vùng 40 (Thể hiện đà giảm đã dừng và phe mua nhập cuộc).

    $Z_{vol} = \frac{V_t - \mu_{V, 20}}{\sigma_{V, 20}}$

    *Điều kiện mua:* $Z_{vol} > 1.5$ (Dòng tiền tổ chức tham gia).
    
4. **Hệ thống Quản trị Rủi ro Tự động:**
    - **Stop-loss (Cắt lỗ):** Mức cắt lỗ cứng là $-5\%$ hoặc giá đóng cửa rớt xuống dưới đường $EMA(20)$.
